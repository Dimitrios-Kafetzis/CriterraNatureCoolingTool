## Part 3 — The cooling archetype set

### Why archetypes

The evidence gates (D-017) reject any performance value without a citation, and
solution-specific cooling literature does not exist for 110 typologies. The
archetype model is how the catalogue is adopted without inventing evidence:
**18 cited evidence classes**, each with a literature-derived °C envelope, and
every catalogue entry mapped to exactly one. An entry inherits a *cited*
envelope and the report states which class it inherited from — so a Miyawaki
forest reports its cooling on the dense-canopy evidence, and says so.

Twelve archetypes are the existing v1.1 typologies, unchanged. Four are new,
retrieved for this release. Two are derived: bounded from an adjacent class with
the bounding argument stated, because the retrieval established that no
class-specific evidence exists.

### What the retrieval found — four results that changed the design

**1. Small constructed water features cool far less than water bodies, and the tool must not interpolate.** Three independent lines converge: `jacobs2020` (16 Dutch configurations, typical 0.2 °C, max 0.6 °C at street level, and a synthesis of 20 papers finding 70% report ≤1 °C), `yao2023b` (field-measured urban pond, 0.6 °C daytime), and `jacobs2020`'s own conclusion that the daytime effect of small water bodies "may be neglected in practice". Lakes cluster near 2 °C; ponds and water squares at 0.2–0.6 °C. Crucially, **the two clusters exist as separate bodies of study, not as a published dose–response curve** — the size-threshold literature is land-surface-temperature only. So this ships as two bands with a hard cut and no interpolation between them.

This corrects an inflation the release would otherwise have introduced: constructed wetlands, retention ponds, detention basins and water squares would naturally have been mapped to `blue_green_corridor` (1.0–3.0 °C) on family resemblance. They map to `small_constructed_water` (0.0–1.0 °C) instead.

Both water archetypes also carry a nocturnal caveat: `yao2023a` and `yao2023b` measure *warming* at night (1.2–1.8 °C), and `ampatzidis2020` reports that bluespace may exacerbate the night-time heat island. The tool is daytime-only and already declares that limitation; these sources sharpen it.

**2. Non-canopy vegetation cools much less than the surface-temperature literature suggests.** `armson2012` is the decisive source because it measures both on the same plots: grass cut *surface* temperature by up to 24 °C but had "little effect upon globe temperatures", while tree shade cut globe temperature by 5–7 °C. `gill2013` adds that grass cooling is reduced or lost under summer drought. Envelope 0.0–1.5 °C, with a floor of genuinely zero for dry or dormant planting.

One correction to carry forward: **`bowler2010` does not contain a quantitative grass-versus-tree figure** — its turf/canopy statement is qualitative and hedged. The v1.1 configuration cites it for exactly that qualitative claim and is therefore sound, but the wording in `nbs_typologies.yaml` ("larger parks and those with trees cooler") should be softened to match the hedge in the source when the library is next edited.

**3. Vegetated shade structures deliver a large comfort benefit and a small air-temperature one.** `ouyang2024` measures the same structures on both axes: air temperature down 1.3–1.4 °C, mean radiant temperature down 14–16 °C — an order of magnitude apart. `chafer2020` is the only measured study of a vine pergola against an identical bare frame (2.5 °C peak, same day). `colter2019` finds sparse canopies perform no better than solid artificial shade.

**The `keravec2026` "shading devices 2.0 °C" figure does not transfer to a single pergola** and is not used for this archetype. That category aggregates area-scale shading where the cooled air volume persists against advection; a pergola shades roughly 10 m². Assigning it 2.0 °C would rate it above tree canopy measured at 1.42 °C. Envelope 0.0–2.5 °C, low confidence, n=1 for the actual intervention.

**4. No evidence exists for productive landscapes, and that is now citable.** `kumar2024` reviews 51 green–blue infrastructure types across 202 publications and explicitly places allotments and city farms among the least-studied categories with insufficient data to quantify. The best allotment measurement (`rost2020`) is nocturnal only. So both productive classes inherit, and the methodology can state the gap as an evidenced finding rather than a search failure — a stronger position than the rain-garden precedent, where no source was found at all.

Irrigation is **not** credited as extra degrees. `cheung2022` shows measured agricultural cooling of 0.09–0.43 °C against modelled potentials of 2.1–2.5 °C, and that irrigation *warms* nights. It is documented as sustaining an entry's position in its envelope through drought, not raising the envelope.

### A validation worth recording

`kumar2024` is a 2024 typology-resolved review that post-dates the v1.1 calibration. It reports street trees at up to 2.8 °C by in-situ monitoring — squarely inside the tool's existing 0.5–3.0 °C envelope. The conservative calibration of D-014 holds up against newer and broader evidence.

It also reports several figures well *above* the tool's envelopes (green walls 4.1 ± 4.2 °C, vegetated balconies 3.8 ± 2.7 °C, botanical gardens 5.0 ± 3.5 °C). **These are deliberately not adopted.** Taking them selectively would break internal consistency — vegetated balconies would outscore street trees — and adopting them wholesale is a full recalibration of the library, which is a separate decision with its own sensitivity analysis. Recommended as a candidate for a future methodology review, not for this release.

## Part 4 — The availability matrix in use

The table above *is* the matrix. This section states the rules it encodes and
shows what they produce.

### Rules

| Assessment scale | What is offered |
|---|---|
| **City** | Ecological-network entries, composed as packages (D-043.2) |
| **District** | District-network entries, composed as packages (D-043.2) |
| **Neighbourhood** | Street, public-space and park entries always; woodland, water-landscape and productive entries when their condition is met |
| **Site** | Neighbourhood-scale entries applicable to the land use, plus all element entries |
| **Building** | Building-scale entries, element entries for the surrounding plot, and the entries the land use maps to |

Conditions, each from a single questionnaire answer:

- **Waterfront type** gates twelve water entries. Four constructed features — constructed wetland, retention pond, detention basin, water square — are gated by *nothing*, per the ruling that constructed features need no existing water body. Two ecological-network entries are also waterfront-gated (river corridor, coastal ecological corridor), which the original rule did not anticipate.
- **Railway** gates exactly one entry, the railway green corridor.
- **Existing woodland** gates only *restoration* types (degraded woodland restoration, reforestation). Creation types — microforest, afforestation, urban woodland, woodland buffer — are ungated, per the ruling.
- **Productive governance** filters the seven productive entries by who delivers them, replacing the yes/no (D-043.3).

**Two observations the numbers force.**

*The site and building menus are large.* 67 options at a school site is not a
list anyone will read. But 49 of those are elements, and elements group cleanly
into five families — tree-based, ground-level, roof, vertical, shelter. Grouped
that way the visible menu is five families plus roughly eighteen composites,
which is navigable. **The picker redesign is therefore not cosmetic: it is what
makes the matrix usable**, and it should group by family with the D-019 fit
annotations preserved.

*The city and district menus are small by construction.* Three and five. Under
D-043.2 these are package components rather than competing alternatives, so the
user composes rather than chooses, and small is correct. Worth confirming this
reads as intended when the picker is built.

## What remains open

The four decisions the brief listed, now sharpened by the mapping:

1. **Do the four new questions feed any score beyond availability?** Recommendation: no. They gate, and gating is not scoring. Making them scoring inputs would need its own justification and would change every existing result.
2. **Is this release a major version bump?** `nbs_type` becomes a list and the input schema gains four fields, which breaks stored drafts. D-029 refuses silent migration, so a stored draft must either be refused with a clear message or migrated explicitly. Recommendation: `v2.0.0`, with drafts migrated by wrapping `nbs_type` into a single-element list and stored results left untouched.
3. **How is a suitability matrix derived where the source gives qualitative limitations only?** Recommendation: inherit the archetype's suitability conditions, then apply per-entry overrides only where the source text states a specific constraint.
4. **Is package size bounded?** Recommendation: no hard cap, but warn above roughly five components, since the capped-never-summed rule means a large package's headline number is carried by its strongest member and the rest add only co-benefit breadth.

On approval of these four, implementation begins: the typology library, the four
questions, the gating rules, package scoring, and the picker redesign — under
the unchanged evidence gates, with the sensitivity analysis regenerated rather
than carried forward, since the library really does change this time.
