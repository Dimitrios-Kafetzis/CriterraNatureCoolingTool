## Part 1 — Structural findings

### The catalogue mixes three kinds of thing

This is the finding everything else follows from. The 243 entries are not 243
comparable options. They are:

1. **Elements** (105 entries) — discrete things you build: a stormwater tree
   pit, an extensive green roof, a modular living wall. These are leaf
   typologies with their own physical form and cooling behaviour.
2. **Composites** (101) — coherent spatial systems *made of* elements: a green
   street, a cooling plaza, a constructed wetland, a neighbourhood park.
3. **Strategies, contexts and models** (37) — not interventions at all: a
   land-use context (hospital landscape), a management objective (climate
   forest), an umbrella category (urban agriculture), a planning model
   (eco-district).

The document says this itself, repeatedly and in its own words. Of the park
list: it "mixes three different bases of classification". Of the ecological
network list: it "mixes several levels of organisation". Of an eco-district: it
"is not itself an NbS". Of urban agriculture: "not one specific NbS typology".

The third kind is where almost all the drops come from, and dropping them is
not a judgement that they do not matter. A dropped land-use context is already
captured by the tool's `land_use` input. A dropped strategy is a *package* of
kept entries — which is exactly what the multi-intervention feature this same
release introduces is for. **Nothing is lost; things move to where they belong.**

### Decision 1 — The four land-use contexts, and a direct conflict with the approved gating rule

The document is explicit: *hospital landscape, campus landscape, memorial
landscape, and schoolyard greening* "describe **land-use contexts**, not NbS
typologies. They become Nature-based Solutions only when they are intentionally
designed to address societal challenges through ecological processes."

This collides with two things at once:

- **The approved building-scale gating rule**, which says that at building
  scale the tool should offer "according to the category of the land use — if
  it is school then schoolyard greening, if healthcare then hospital landscape".
  That rule assumes these four exist as selectable solutions.
- **The tool as it stands.** `schoolyard_greening` is one of the fourteen
  literature-grounded typologies shipped since v1, and `land_use` already
  offers *school*, *healthcare*, *campus* and *memorial* as answers — the last
  two added in v1.1 last week, at your request.

So the tool would ask "what is the land use here?" and then offer, as an
intervention, the land use it was just told about.

**Recommendation: treat all four as contexts, not typologies, and let the
gating rule do the work it was actually written to do.** The intent behind the
rule — that a school site should be offered the interventions that suit a
school — is better served by mapping the land use to the *real* typologies that
fit it (tree plaza, courtyard greening, pocket park, permeable plaza, edible
schoolyard planting) than by offering one pseudo-typology named after the site.
The user gets more, not less, and the availability matrix in part 4 is where
that mapping is written down.

The cost is real and must be stated: `schoolyard_greening` would be retired as
a typology. It carries cited values and appears in golden scenario s06. It
would become a land-use-driven *bundle* of kept typologies, which is a
methodology change with a version bump, not a rename.

**If you prefer to keep them**, that is a defensible position — you may simply
want a planner who selects "school" to see a card that says "schoolyard
greening" — and the pack can be re-cut that way. But it should be a decision
taken knowingly against the source document, and recorded as such.

### Decision 2 — The district scale is nearly empty

Under the approved rule "for the district must be only the district scale", a
district-scale user would see **five** options, and four of them carry a flag:

| Entry | Concern |
|---|---|
| 10.2 Blue-Green Infrastructure Network | sound |
| 10.3 Urban Cooling Network | sound — the strongest district entry for this tool |
| 10.1 Green Infrastructure Network | is really the umbrella over 10.2–10.4 |
| 10.4 Green Mobility Network | components overlap 10.3 almost entirely |
| 10.10 Forest District | depends on the urban-forest ruling (decision 4) |

Everything else at district scale is a strategy the document disqualifies:
sponge district, climate adaptation district, eco-district, green campus,
productive district, resilient waterfront district, climate-resilient
regeneration district. A strict reading leaves **three** usable options.

The same applies one scale up: city scale offers only the six kept
ecological-network entries.

**Recommendation: let city and district scales offer packages, not single
interventions.** This is what those scales *are* — a district is not a place
where you install one thing. A district-scale assessment should compose several
kept typologies and score them as a package, which is precisely the machinery
this release is already building for comment 8. The five district "networks"
then become **named preset packages** rather than pseudo-typologies, and the
thin menu stops being a problem and becomes the correct model.

This is a change to the approved gating rule, so it needs your ruling.

### Decision 3 — The productive-landscape gating question is the wrong shape

The approved question is "is there interest to create productive landscape from
individuals or communities", yes or no. But of the seven kept productive
entries, only four are individual- or community-created:

| Governance | Entries |
|---|---|
| Community | community garden, urban orchard, food forest |
| Individual | allotment garden |
| Institutional | school garden |
| Commercial | urban farm, agroforestry system |

A "no" answer would suppress the urban farm and the agroforestry system —
which are the highest-canopy, highest-cooling entries in the group, and the
ones a municipality is most likely to deliver.

**Recommendation: ask who could deliver it, not whether individuals want it.**
A multi-select over {community, individual, institutional, commercial} gates
correctly and costs the user no more effort than a yes/no. If you prefer to
keep a single yes/no, it should be reworded to "is productive landscape
appropriate on this site?" and gate the whole group rather than filter within it.

### Decision 4 — "Urban forest" means two different things

The document warns that urban forest may mean either "the complete urban tree
and woodland system of a city" or "a specific tree-dominated intervention
site", and that "these two meanings should not be mixed". The tool's existing
`urban_forest` typology uses the second meaning, with a 2 000 m² minimum area —
so it is already the site reading.

**Recommendation: keep the site reading, and rename the entry to make it
unambiguous** — *urban woodland (site)* or similar. The city-wide reading is
then what a city-scale package expresses, consistent with decision 2. This is a
display-name change, not a methodology change, provided the envelope and
minimum area stay put.

### Decision 5 — Street typologies are compositions, and that is an opportunity

The document is emphatic that street-scale typologies are "integrated linear
systems" composed of element-scale interventions, that the elements "should
simply be referenced rather than redefined", and that "Street Trees" must be
removed as a street typology because it is an element.

The tool's existing `street_tree_planting` typology is exactly the entry the
document says to remove.

**Recommendation: model the seven kept street typologies as preset packages
over element entries** — the same mechanism as decisions 1 and 2 — and keep
`street_tree_planting` as the *element*, which is what it has always really
been. This makes the catalogue self-consistent: elements are what you build,
everything above them composes elements.

Note the pattern across decisions 1, 2 and 5: three independent findings all
resolve through the package machinery. That is a good sign the package feature
is the right structural answer and not merely a requested feature.

### Decision 6 — Four catalogue entries land on existing tool typologies

Four merges target the current fourteen rather than another catalogue entry:
cooling park → `park_upgrade`, riparian forest → `riparian_restoration`,
productive courtyard → `courtyard_greening`, edible streetscape →
`street_tree_planting`. Two more overlap heavily: school garden against
`schoolyard_greening`, river park against `blue_green_corridor`.

**Recommendation: the existing fourteen keep their identity and their cited
values, and absorb the catalogue entries as variants.** They are the only
entries in the entire library with individually retrieved literature behind
them, and discarding that to adopt a catalogue name would trade evidence for
tidiness. Where a catalogue entry adds a genuinely distinct behaviour rather
than a name, it stays separate.

### What these six decisions change downstream

| Decision | Affects |
|---|---|
| 1 — land-use contexts | Availability matrix; possible retirement of `schoolyard_greening`; golden scenario s06 |
| 2 — city/district packages | The approved gating rule; the package feature's scope |
| 3 — productive governance | One questionnaire field's type and options |
| 4 — urban forest naming | One display name |
| 5 — street as packages | How street typologies are represented; `street_tree_planting`'s status |
| 6 — existing fourteen | Which entries carry cited values and which inherit |

Decisions 1, 2 and 5 also determine how many archetypes part 3 needs, which is
why parts 3 and 4 wait.

## What happens after your ruling

1. **Parts 3 and 4 of the pack** — the cooling archetype set with retrieved
   citations, the entry-to-archetype mapping, and the full availability matrix
   (assessment scale × site conditions × land use → entries). These are written
   against whatever the six decisions above settle.
2. Then, and only then, configuration and code: the typology library, the four
   new site questions, the gating rules, the package scoring, and the picker
   redesign — under the unchanged evidence gates, with the sensitivity analysis
   regenerated rather than carried forward, since this time the library really
   does change.
